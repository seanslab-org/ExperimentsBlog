Story

Six circles and two rounded rectangles. Nothing else. The big round butt is the dominant mass. The head is a smaller circle peeking out behind it. The trunk is a tapering cascade of three dots. The eye is a small cutout in the head. The legs are two thin rectangles dropping below the body.

Adjacent pairs of circles are tangent with a small overlap so the silhouette reads as a single body rather than loose parts. The trunk reads as a curl because its three circles trace a 90° clockwise rotation: 135° from the head, then 45°, then −45°. That is the only thing making the curl a curl.

Bonus · the constellation

Take only the center point of each shape — the six circles and the two legs. Plot them on a dark sky. The result is an asterism: a five-star curl at the top (trunk and head), one bright star in the middle (the body), and two feet at the base. We named it Elephantulus — the little elephant. A calf glancing back, held still in starlight.
